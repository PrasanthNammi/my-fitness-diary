# Cycle & Health: questions for a clinician

For a gynaecologist or endocrinologist reviewing MFD's Cycle & Health section before it is offered more widely. Each
item says what the app does now and what we would like confirmed or corrected. Sources are in EVIDENCE.md.

## Definitions and wording

1. **Cycle and period**: a cycle starts on the first day of a period; spotting, withdrawal bleeding and "unsure" never
   start one; a period day within 7 days of the last belongs to the same period. Is the 7-day rule sensible for
   people with PCOS (irregular, sometimes prolonged bleeding)?
2. **Normal range**: Insights shades 24–38 days (FIGO 2018) and calls cycles varying by more than 7–9 days
   "irregular, a pattern to show your doctor, not a diagnosis". Is this wording appropriate?
3. **Symptoms list** (fatigue, cramps, acne, bloating, headache, hair changes, cold intolerance, palpitations…): are
   any missing that matter for PCOS or thyroid follow-up?

## The estimate

4. The next-period estimate is an 80% range from the person's own cycles (method of Li et al. 2021 / Urteaga et al.
   2021, tested on app users aged 21–33 without hormonal contraception; not on PCOS or thyroid conditions). It needs
   3 full cycles and shows her own track record. Is it acceptable to show it at all for PCOS, and is the explanation
   clear enough?
5. Should the estimate be hidden while someone records hormonal contraception, pregnancy or breastfeeding?

## Lab results and medicines

6. Thyroid results are shown with each lab's printed range, never interpreted. The ⓘ says results can be affected
   by timing, a recent levothyroxine dose, illness and biotin. Correct and sufficient?
7. Levothyroxine reminders are set at the person's chosen time. Should the app mention taking it apart from food or
   other medicines, or is that better left to the doctor?
8. Home LH tests are recorded only (no fertile days), because LH is often raised in PCOS. Please confirm.

## Possible warnings (not built; only with your approval)

9. Which situations, if any, should prompt "consider talking to a doctor", and with what wording? For example:
   no period for 90 days; bleeding longer than 8 days; very heavy flow (soaking a pad or tampon every hour); severe
   pain (8–10) on several days; bleeding after menopause.
10. Any situations where the app should show urgent advice (e.g. heavy bleeding with dizziness)?

## Report for the doctor

11. The report lists cycles (start, length, bleeding days, heaviest flow, most pain), conditions, medicines with
    doses taken/missed in the last 90 days, lab results with lab ranges, and frequent symptoms. What would you add
    or remove to make it useful in a 10-minute appointment?
